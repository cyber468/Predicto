The research behind it

Predicto does not invent its own test. Here is what each part rests on, and where it stops.

The statements are a public research scale

They come from the International Personality Item Pool (IPIP), a public-domain collection of personality items started by Lewis Goldberg. The 50-statement Big Five set builds on his work on Big Five markers. IPIP items have been translated into more than 25 languages.

Goldberg (1992); Goldberg et al. (2006)

Five traits, found again and again

Decades of work on how people describe each other kept producing the same five broad dimensions, and Digman's 1990 review traced how that five-factor model emerged. A large comparison by Roberts and colleagues reported that traits predicted outcomes such as mortality, divorce and occupational attainment about as well as socioeconomic status and cognitive ability.

Digman (1990); Roberts et al. (2007)

Your numbers use the published scoring key

Each statement is rated 1 to 5. The negatively worded ones are flipped, the ten statements for each trait are averaged, and the result is rescaled to 0 to 100%. The IPIP notes that scoring five response options from 1 to 5 keeps results comparable with published studies.

IPIP 50-item scale and key

The group profile is a pattern, not a diagnosis

A K-means model with five clusters was trained on about one million people's answers to these same statements. Your answers are matched to the nearest cluster, and the filled shape shows that cluster's average. The five groups describe that sample. They are not five kinds of people, which is why your own dashed outline is the more personal result. The training data is not part of this project, so it cannot be checked here.

What this cannot tell you

  • A score is an average, not a rank. 68% means your answers averaged about 3.4 out of 5. It does not mean you beat 68% of people.
  • Ten statements per trait is a short form. It is quick but less precise than long inventories, and some independent analyses find the 50-statement set's structure less clean than longer versions, especially for Openness.
  • It is self-report. Your mood and the day you answer can move the scores. Retake it another day to see how stable they are.
  • The comparison group is unknown to us. It is people who answered the same questionnaire, and we have no detail on who they were.
  • It is not a clinical or hiring tool. Do not use it to diagnose anyone or to make decisions about people.
